Correlation vs Covariance — Easy Explanation
1. Covariance

Covariance tells us the direction in which two variables move.

Suppose we have:

Study hours
Exam marks

If study hours increase and marks also increase:

Study Hours ↑
       ↓
Marks ↑

They move in the same direction → Positive covariance.

If study hours increase but marks decrease:

Study Hours ↑
       ↓
Marks ↓

They move in opposite directions → Negative covariance.

So:

Positive covariance → same direction
Negative covariance → opposite direction
Zero/near zero covariance → no clear linear movement
2. Correlation

Correlation tells us the direction AND how strong the relationship is.

Its value is always:

$$ -1 \leq r \leq 1 $$

For example:

r = +0.9 → strong positive relationship
r = +0.2 → weak positive relationship
r = -0.8 → strong negative relationship
r = 0 → no linear relationship

So correlation gives us more understandable information about the relationship.

3. Main Difference

Imagine two variables:

X = Study Hours
Y = Marks

If students who study more generally get higher marks:

Covariance:
→ tells us positive direction.

Correlation:
→ tells us positive direction + strength.

For example:

Correlation = +0.95

This means the relationship is strong and positive.

4. Why correlation is easier to use?

Covariance doesn't have a fixed range.

It could be:

10
100
5000
-200

So comparing covariance values can be difficult.

Correlation is always between:

-1 ←──── 0 ────→ +1

Therefore, it's easier to understand and compare.

🧠 One-line revision

Covariance tells the direction of relationship, while correlation tells both the direction and strength of a linear relationship.
